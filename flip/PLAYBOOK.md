# What to flip with $50

You asked me to decide. Here it is.

## The pick: small, light, and easy to identify

Not "valuable." **Small.** At this size of bankroll, the postage costs as much
as the platform fees, and it comes straight off your profit. The same item
that nets $12.48 in a box nets $16.23 in a padded envelope — 30% more money
for zero extra work or risk.

So the rule that beats every "what's hot" list: **if it doesn't fit in a
padded envelope, it needs a very good reason.**

That points at: trading cards, LEGO minifigures, small retired LEGO sets,
jewellery, watch straps, phone accessories, small collectibles, sheet music,
pins and badges.

**Start with LEGO minifigures.** Reasons, in order:

1. Nearly impossible to fake convincingly, and easy to check against photos.
2. Public price history exists (BrickLink, BrickEconomy), so you are reading
   prices rather than guessing them.
3. Condition is binary-ish — it's complete or it isn't. No grading skill needed.
4. They cost $5–30, so $50 actually buys you several. Diversification matters
   more than any single win.
5. Envelope-sized. See above.

**Avoid at this size:** clothing (slow, bulky, sizing returns), furniture
(shipping kills it), anything electronic (returns and "not as described"
disputes), and anything needing expertise to authenticate. Sneakers and
designer goods have the best headline margins and the worst counterfeit risk —
skip them until you have losses you can afford.

## Before every single purchase

```
python3 flip/flip.py maxbid --sell <what it really sells for> --ship <postage> --want 12
```

That gives you a number. Do not pay more than it. This is the entire discipline
and it is the only thing separating this from a hobby that loses money.

"What it really sells for" means **completed, actually-sold prices** — not what
people are asking. Asking prices are fiction; half of them never sell.

## The honest expectations

With $50 you hold about 3 items. One bad buy is a third of your stock, so the
first cycle is tuition, not income. Realistic outcome if you're disciplined:
roughly $40/month, doubling your stake in about two months.

That is genuinely good as a percentage and genuinely small as an amount. It
stays small until you put more money in — which is the honest thing nobody
selling a flipping course will tell you.

## The one rule that keeps you out of trouble

**Never buy something you can't check the sold price of.** If you can't look up
what it actually sold for, you aren't flipping, you're gambling. Walk away.
