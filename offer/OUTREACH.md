# Outreach

## The mechanic

Do not pitch. **Run the free teaser first, unasked, and lead with their number.**
A specific, true, slightly alarming fact about their own company is the only
cold open that reliably gets replies.

Pick 20 targets where you already have a weak tie — a former colleague, someone
whose post you commented on, a founder in a shared Slack. Zero cold email. Zero
ad spend. This is the whole cycle-1 distribution plan.

## The message

> Subject: you show up in 15% of AI answers about [category]
>
> [Name] — I built something that measures whether AI assistants recommend a
> brand, and I ran [Company] through it out of curiosity.
>
> Across 20 questions a buyer would actually type, asked 5 times each:
> you got named in 15%. [Competitor] got named in 71%.
>
> The part I'd want to see if it were my company is the blind-spot list —
> 6 questions where three competitors come up and you never do.
>
> Want the full report? Happy to just send it.

Then send it. Free, no conditions.

**The ask comes after they react**, not before: "If it's useful I'm doing these
properly for $149 — 20 questions, 100 samples, tracked quarterly. Want one for
[their other product line / their main competitor]?"

## Why free-first beats a paywall here

At $149 the bottleneck is belief, not price. Nobody has seen this measured
before, so nobody knows what they are buying. The teaser converts belief, and
it costs you well under a dollar in tokens. Spending $0.60 to open a $149
conversation is the best trade in the whole plan.

## The rule that keeps this honest

If ten people get a free teaser and not one replies asking for more, the offer
is wrong — not the outreach volume. Change the offer or stop. Do not respond by
sending fifty more.
